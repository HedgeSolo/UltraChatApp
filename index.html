Cellular Respiration & Bioenergetics in Eukaryotic Organisms

Advanced Molecular Biology • Research Analysis • Department of Biological Sciences

1. Introduction & Glycolytic Pathways

Cellular respiration represents the fundamental metabolic framework by which aerobic cells harvest biochemical energy from nutrient molecules. Adenosine triphosphate (ATP) serves as the universal energetic currency, facilitating anabolic synthesis, transmembrane ion transport, and biomechanical work across eukaryotic biological systems.

The initial phase, glycolysis, occurs within the cytosol under anaerobic conditions. Glucose, a six-carbon hexose sugar, undergoes a ten-step enzymatic cleavage resulting in two triose pyruvate molecules, generating a net yield of 2 ATP and 2 NADH per glucose substrate.

2. The Krebs Cycle & Mitochondrial Matrix Translocation

Upon transport across the mitochondrial inner membrane via pyruvate translocase, pyruvate undergoes oxidative decarboxylation catalyzed by the multi-enzyme pyruvate dehydrogenase complex. This conversion generates Acetyl-CoA, which enters the Citric Acid Cycle (Krebs Cycle) within the matrix.

Through consecutive oxidation steps, high-energy electron carriers (NADH and FADH2) are produced alongside metabolic carbon dioxide exhaled as metabolic waste.

Key Takeaway Note:

Oxidative phosphorylation accounts for approximately 88% of total ATP synthesis within mammalian hepatocytes.
